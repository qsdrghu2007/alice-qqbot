## 1. Current Scale Limit

The only scale number is:

| Name | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `MAX_AUTHORIZED_USERS` | **3** |

"Authorized Users" means the different natural persons currently allowed
to access a hosted, shared or interactive deployment. Administrators
count. Count all members who can interact through a QQ group or other
shared conversation. The metric is current authorized users, not monthly
active users.

All deployments, instances, channels and accounts operated by the same
operator, controlled by the same organization or people, used for the
same beneficiary, or serving a coordinated purpose are aggregated. This
limit cannot be increased by creating smaller instances, groups, account
shells or intermediary operators.

A person who only downloads the source, reads it, or runs it locally
without making the service available to another person does not count as
another Authorized User. Ordinary local personal use is one user.

## 2. Closed Access

The deployment must remain closed to an unspecified public. It must not
provide public registration, self-service admission, an unrestricted
public bot, or service to an open public group.

An allowlist or private invitation is useful evidence of intent but does
not make a deployment closed if other people can actually access it.
Publishing the source code in a public repository does not by itself make
the hosted service public.

## 3. Noncommercial Requirement

The use must not be Commercial Use as defined in
`LICENSES/ALICE-SOURCE-AVAILABLE.md`. In particular, the use may not:

- charge fees, subscriptions or paid access;
- involve advertising, promotion, sponsorship, lead generation, sale of
  products or services, traffic acquisition or data monetization;
- be included in a commercial product, paid service or commercial hosted
  service;
- support a free service used to attract customers or another revenue
  generating product;
- serve a business or organization's production environment or ordinary
  internal operations, even without a fee; or
- be performed for an employer, business sponsor or organization's
  commercial purpose.

Ordinary personal study, experimentation, hobbies and student projects
qualify when all conditions are met. A closed course or research project
at a school or nonprofit institution may qualify when it has no
commercial purpose and no more than three Authorized Users. Ordinary
tuition or a noncommercial research grant does not by itself make the
course or research commercial.

A nonprofit organization's ordinary production or business operations do
not qualify merely because it is nonprofit. A public community instance
does not qualify merely because it is free or nonprofit.

An employee's private, unpaid hobby may qualify when it is unrelated to
the employer's business and does not provide a direct or indirect
commercial benefit to the employer.

Voluntary donations qualify only when the donor receives no advertising,
promotion, priority access, data benefit or other consideration, the
operator does not distribute profits, and the funds cover verified direct
hosting or operating costs without exceeding them. A simple supporter
acknowledgement is allowed when it is not promotion or advertising.
